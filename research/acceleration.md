# Work in progress: making FHE faster

Checked 2026-10-17. Sources are linked inline. "UNVERIFIED" marks what I could not confirm from a primary source. Zama's benchmark numbers come from the current TFHE-rs docs (v1.8 era); the tables are SVG images, and I extracted their text directly.

## Summary (slide-ready)

- **GPU: about 5–10× lower latency, and more throughput.** On 8×H100, an encrypted `FheUint8 > FheUint8` takes **4.85 ms**, against 15.1 ms on a 192-core EPYC server ([GPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-integer-operations), [CPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-integer-operations)). Our MacBook takes 41 ms. The 8×H100 node does ~8× better, a factor of 10, not 10^9. *(measured, by Zama)*
- **FPGA ships today.** Zama's open-source HPU runs on an AMD Alveo V80 at ~13–14k bootstraps/s and roughly matches 8×H100 on 64-bit ops ([announcement](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-hpu-on-fpga-the-first-open-source-hardware-accelerator-for-fhe), [docs](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks)). *(measured)*
- **ASIC: 1,000–5,000× is claimed on research silicon.** Intel's Heracles (DARPA DPRIVE, ISSCC 2026, 3 nm) is "1,074 to 5,547 times as fast" as a Xeon on seven FHE primitives. It is a research chip with "no stated commercial plans" ([IEEE Spectrum, 2026-03-10](https://spectrum.ieee.org/fhe-intel)). Niobium's commercial ASIC is still in development ([SEMIFIVE, 2026-02-19](https://semifive.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/semifive-partners-with-niobium-to-develop-fhe-accelerator-driving-u-s-market-expansion/)). *(measured on a demo, vendor-reported)*
- **Algorithms gained ~50× in under 10 years.** One TFHE bootstrap went from 690 ms to 13 ms in 2016 ([ePrint 2018/421](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/421)), and from 53 ms (CPU, 2021) to **945 µs** on one H100 in 2025 ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/bootstrapping-tfhe-ciphertexts-in-less-than-one-millisecond)).
- **Bottom line: hardware buys about 10^1 (GPU) to a claimed 10^3 (ASIC). Our gap is 10^9.** FHE becomes usable for small, high-value computations. It stays far from plaintext speed. DARPA's goal was "within a factor of ten of unencrypted"; the program is "now complete" ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/data-protection-in-virtual-environments)), and I found no public result that reached it (UNVERIFIED either way).

## 1. GPUs

- **TFHE-rs CUDA backend, latency** (8×H100 SXM5, `PARAM_GPU_MULTI_BIT_GROUP_4_MESSAGE_2_CARRY_2_KS_PBS`, p-fail 2^-128) ([GPU integer page](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-integer-operations)):
  - `FheUint8`: add 5.09 ms, mul 9.88 ms, gt 4.85 ms.
  - `FheUint64`: add 9.52 ms, mul 31.9 ms, gt 9.08 ms, div 529 ms.
- **CPU reference, same ops** (AWS hpc8a.96xlarge, 2×96-core AMD EPYC 9R45, `PARAM_MESSAGE_2_CARRY_2_KS_PBS_TUNIFORM_2M128`) ([CPU integer page](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-integer-operations)):
  - `FheUint8`: add 25.4 ms, mul 44.6 ms, gt 15.1 ms.
  - `FheUint64`: add 61.3 ms, mul 262 ms, gt 49.7 ms, div 3.36 s.
- **Throughput is less clear-cut.** By Zama's own throughput tables, the 192-core CPU beats 8×H100 on small types: `FheUint8` gt runs at 2.55k ops/s on the CPU against 480 ops/s on the GPU ([CPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-integer-operations), [GPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-integer-operations)). On 64-bit ops the GPU wins: mul is 53.2 against 5.66 ops/s. The pages don't explain the methodology. On a full confidential-token transfer (ERC7984), 8×H100 does 885–1,040 transfers/s ([GPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-erc7984)), against 14–24/s on the CPU ([CPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-erc7984)).
- **Bootstrap (PBS), 4-bit precision**: on H100, classical 4.21 ms and multi-bit 1.05 ms ([GPU PBS](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-programmable-bootstrapping)). On the CPU, 5.64 ms and 2.65 ms ([CPU PBS](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-programmable-bootstrapping)). Zama reports 945 µs on one H100 and 189,000 bootstraps/s on 8×H100 (2025-09-17, [blog](https://www.zama.org/post/bootstrapping-tfhe-ciphertexts-in-less-than-one-millisecond)).
- **Zama Protocol targets** ([litepaper](https://docs.zama.org/protocol/zama-protocol-litepaper)): "over 20 transactions per second on CPU" today (measured, vendor), "500-1000 TPS per chain" on GPU by end of 2026 (**target**; whether it was met is UNVERIFIED), "100,000+ tps / chain on a single server" with ASICs (**projection**, no date found). Zama's framing: "FHE is no longer limited by underlying algorithms, and is now mostly driven by Moore's law."
- **CKKS on GPU** (FIDESlib, ISPASS 2025): bootstrapping (64 slots, 13 levels remaining) takes 73.5 ms on an RTX 4090. OpenFHE with Intel HEXL takes 5,204 ms on 24 threads of a Ryzen 9 7900 ([arXiv 2507.04775, Table VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04775v1)). Earlier, Jung et al. reported "over 100x" for GPU bootstrapping and found it memory-bound ([TCHES 2021](https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/view/9062); see `history-and-bottlenecks.md`).

## 2. FPGA and dedicated hardware

- **Zama HPU** (announced 2025-05-21 with TFHE-rs v1.2): an "open-source hardware accelerator", a full processor rather than a bootstrapping block. It runs on an AMD Alveo V80 (7 nm FPGA, 2×HBM2e) at 350 MHz, ~200 W, "around 13k PBS/s", and was "not fully production-ready yet" ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-hpu-on-fpga-the-first-open-source-hardware-accelerator-for-fhe)). The current docs measure up to 14,167 KS-PBS/s in batches of 12, at 847 µs latency ([HPU PBS](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/hpu/hpu-programmable-bootstrapping)), and `FheUint64` add 8.48 ms, mul 121 ms, gt 7.05 ms on one V80 ([summary](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks)). An ERC7984 transfer peaks at 103 ops/s on one HPU ([HPU ERC7984](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/hpu/hpu-erc7984)).
- **DARPA DPRIVE**: the goal was to "enable FHE computation within a factor of ten of unencrypted computations". The program "is now complete" ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/data-protection-in-virtual-environments)). Performers were Intel (Heracles), Duality (TREBUCHET, $14.5M, [PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/duality-technologies-awarded-14-5m-darpa-contract-to-develop-worlds-fastest-privacy-preserving-hardware-accelerator-301221040.html)), Galois (BASALISC, $15.3M, [Galois](https://www.galois.com/articles/galois-basalisc-project-wins-15-3m-darpa-contract)) and SRI ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/darpa-funds-asic-accelerators-for-fully-homomorphic-encryption/)). I found no published silicon results for TREBUCHET or BASALISC (UNVERIFIED).
- **Intel Heracles** (ISSCC 2026): 3 nm FinFET, 64 tile-pairs at 1.2 GHz, ~200 mm², 2×24 GB HBM at 819 GB/s. It runs 1,074–5,547× faster than a Xeon on seven FHE operations, and 2,355× on the NTT. A demo private ballot lookup took 15 ms on a Xeon and 14 µs on Heracles. It is a research demonstration with "no stated commercial plans" ([IEEE Spectrum, 2026-03-10](https://spectrum.ieee.org/fhe-intel)). The comparison is against a CPU, not a GPU, and covers lattice primitives, not TFHE-rs integer ops.
- **Niobium**: taped out a first chip (GlobalFoundries 12 nm) and claims "1000-5000×" over CPUs ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/niobuim-raises-5-5-million-tapes-out-fhe-chip/), **claim**). Its production ASIC is on Samsung 8LPU with SEMIFIVE, a contract signed 2026-02-19, with no availability date ([SEMIFIVE](https://semifive.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/semifive-partners-with-niobium-to-develop-fhe-accelerator-driving-u-s-market-expansion/)).
- **Optalysys (optical)**: its shipped product, LightLocker Node (June 2025), claims "over 100 encrypted transactions per second" and "40% less energy than traditional GPUs" ([Startups Magazine, 2025-06-16](https://startupsmagazine.co.uk/article-optalysys-launches-hardware-solution-blockchain-transactions), secondary). A search snippet of optalysys.com calls it "FPGA-based", i.e. not photonic, but I could not open the page to confirm (UNVERIFIED). The photonic chips are still in development: a seventh generation ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/fhe-intel)), and a first photonics system due to a US partner after a £23M round in Jan 2026 ([BeBeez](https://bebeez.eu/2026/01/22/leeds-uk-based-photonic-chip-startup-optalysys-raises-23m/), secondary).
- **Shipped ASIC: none found.** In Dec 2023, IEEE Spectrum expected Cornami and Optalysys products and Fabric Cryptography mass production in 2024 ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/homomorphic-encryption)). As of Oct 2026 I found no FHE ASIC on sale (UNVERIFIED: an absence is hard to prove).

## 3. Algorithms, compilers, standards

- **TFHE bootstrapping history**: "from 690ms to 13ms single core, using 16MB bootstrapping key instead of 1GB" (Chillotti et al., J. Cryptology; [ePrint 2018/421](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/421); the ASIACRYPT 2016 version says "less than 0.1 seconds", [ePrint 2016/870](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/870)). Today it is 945 µs on one H100, a 56× gain over Zama's 2021 CPU figure of 53 ms ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/bootstrapping-tfhe-ciphertexts-in-less-than-one-millisecond)).
- **Multi-bit PBS**: it processes several key bits per step, which gives more parallelism ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/bootstrapping-tfhe-ciphertexts-in-less-than-one-millisecond)). It is ~2× faster on the CPU at 4-bit precision (2.65 ms against 5.64 ms) and ~4× on the GPU (1.05 ms against 4.21 ms) ([CPU PBS](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-programmable-bootstrapping), [GPU PBS](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-programmable-bootstrapping)).
- **Drift** (Bernard, Joye, Smart, Walter; Zama; EUROCRYPT 2025): a redefined modulus switch that reaches IND-CPA^D-grade failure probabilities without much larger parameters, with "no noticeable performance impact" ([ePrint 2024/1718](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1718)). It is a security gain at a near-zero cost, not a speed-up.
- **Compilers**: Google's **HEIR**, built on MLIR, aims to support "all mainstream techniques in homomorphic encryption" and the main libraries and accelerators ([arXiv 2508.11095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11095), [GitHub](https://github.com/google/heir)). Zama's **Concrete** is "an open-source FHE Compiler that simplifies the use of" FHE ([docs](https://docs.zama.org/concrete)). Both target usability and hardware portability more than raw speed.
- **Standards**: ISO/IEC 28033 (JTC 1/SC 27). Part 2 (exact arithmetic, BGV/BFV family) was published in Jan 2026 ([BSI, identical adoption](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/bs-iso-iec-28033-2-information-security-fully-homomorphic-encryption-part-2-mechanisms-for-exact-arithmetic-on-modular-integers)). Part 3 covers approximate numbers (CKKS) ([DIN](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/draft-standard/iso-iec-dis-28033-3/398741185)). Part 4 (look-up tables, "CGGI and its variants", i.e. TFHE) is at FDIS stage 50.20 ([ISS mirror of ISO project](https://iss.rs/en/project/show/iso:proj:87641)). The BGV/BFV scheme mapping of Part 2 is UNVERIFIED (inferred from the title).

## 4. Synthesis: how much of the 10^9 can hardware recover?

- **Our numbers**: an 8-bit comparison takes 41 ms on the MacBook Air (4 threads), 15.1 ms on a 192-core server, and 4.85 ms on 8×H100. That is ~8× from a GPU node, and ~3× from a server-class CPU alone ([CPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/cpu/cpu-integer-operations), [GPU](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks/gpu/gpu-integer-operations)). An FPGA lands in the same range ([summary](https://docs.zama.org/tfhe-rs/get-started/benchmarks)).
- **Measured, today**: GPU and FPGA give ~10× on TFHE latency and ~70× on CKKS bootstrapping (FIDESlib). Throughput scales with more hardware, because independent ciphertexts parallelise. Latency barely does: the chain of bootstraps inside one comparison is sequential.
- **Claimed, on research or first-gen silicon**: ASICs claim 10^3 to 5×10^3 over CPU (Heracles measured on primitives; Niobium vendor claim). Even if the full claim carried over to our workload, ~10^9 / 5×10^3 ≈ **2×10^5 slower than plaintext would remain**.
- **Projected**: Zama's ASIC target is 100,000+ TPS, i.e. ~5,000× today's 20 TPS CPU. It is consistent with those ASIC claims, but no date was found.
- **Careful wording for the slide**: "Hardware closes 3 of the 9 orders of magnitude, on paper." DPRIVE's "within 10×" goal referred to its own benchmark workloads, not to a sub-nanosecond plaintext loop like ours, and no public result shows it was reached (UNVERIFIED). The realistic message: FHE goes from "impossible" to "affordable for small, high-value computations", not to "free".
